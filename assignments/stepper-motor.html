Stepper Motor Project

Innovator Journal | Unit 2

Project Overview

For this project, Ava and I worked on setting up and controlling a stepper motor using an Arduino, a breadboard, and an A4988 motor driver. We followed a tutorial to learn how the different components connected and worked together. Our goal was to build a working circuit that could make the stepper motor move.

1. Initial Setup

We started by following a tutorial and assembling the different parts of the circuit. We had to figure out where each component belonged and how to connect everything correctly.

This was our first attempt at wiring the circuit. Since there were several components and connections to keep track of, we needed to pay attention to the tutorial and make sure the wires were in the correct places.

The A4988 driver is an important part of the circuit because it helps control the electrical current going to the stepper motor. The heatsink helps dissipate heat from the driver during operation.

2. Troubleshooting Our Circuit

After our first attempt, we realized that our circuit was not working because we had made mistakes in the wiring. Instead of continuing with a setup that did not work, we needed to figure out what had gone wrong.

We looked at John Deng and Ryan's working setup to compare it with ours and better understand the connections. After realizing that we had wired our circuit incorrectly, we took apart our original setup and decided to start over. Looking at a working example gave us a better idea of what we needed to change.

3. Getting Help During Office Hours

During office hours, Dr. Dzula helped me understand how the breadboard connections worked and how to wire the circuit correctly. Instead of randomly changing wires, I began to understand where the connections needed to go and how the different parts of the circuit worked together.

After about an hour of working with Dr. Dzula, I finally had a working setup. With his help, I was able to correct the wiring and better understand how the breadboard and driver were being used in the circuit.

4. Final Result

Seeing the stepper motor finally move showed me that the circuit was working. This was the result of rebuilding the setup, correcting our mistakes, and getting help to understand the wiring.

5. What I Learned

One of the biggest things I learned was how a breadboard actually works. I learned that the holes on a breadboard are connected in specific groups, rather than every hole being connected to every other hole. Some sections need wires to connect them to other parts of the circuit. Understanding this helped me see why placing a wire in the wrong row could stop the entire circuit from working.

I also learned more about the A4988 driver. The Arduino sends control signals, while the driver helps control the current supplied to the stepper motor's coils so it can move in steps. This helped me understand that every component has a specific job and that the components need to work together for the circuit to function.

Another important lesson was how much accurate wiring matters. Even when all the correct components are being used, the circuit will not work if the connections are wrong. When our first setup failed, comparing it with a working example and asking Dr. Dzula for help taught me to troubleshoot more carefully instead of just guessing.

I also learned that taking something apart and starting over can be a useful part of the engineering process. Although our first setup did not work, rebuilding it helped me understand the connections better. I learned that observing other people's designs, asking for help, and understanding why a solution works are more useful than simply copying the final result.

Overall, this project helped me become more confident with breadboards, motor drivers, and electronic circuits. Most importantly, I learned that getting a project to work is not just about following instructions. It is also about understanding the system, identifying mistakes, and learning from the process.
