Day 13 — Assignment: Innovator Journal Entry on Simple Spinning Scanner

In this project, Ava and I worked with an Arduino Uno, an A4988 stepper motor driver, and a stepper motor to learn how to create a simple spinning scanner. We followed a tutorial, experimented with the hardware and wiring, and worked through several problems before getting the system to function.

Process

1. Getting Started

Ava and I began by following a YouTube tutorial on how to control and operate a stepper motor using an Arduino. We used the tutorial to understand the purpose of the different components and how they were supposed to work together.

2. Following the Tutorial

We continued assembling the circuit and following the tutorial's instructions. At this stage, we focused on understanding the connections between the Arduino, breadboard, driver, and other components needed to control the stepper motor.

3. Adding a Heat Sink

We attached a heat sink to the driver's main chip to help dissipate heat and prevent the component from overheating during operation.

4. Reaching a Roadblock

At this point, we realized that we had reached a roadblock. The system had been assembled incorrectly on the breadboard, and we were unsure how to proceed. After Ava left, I continued troubleshooting on my own. I disassembled the setup and used ChatGPT to help me understand how the components should be connected and operated, but my initial attempt still did not work.

5. Using a Working Setup as a Reference

John Deng and Ryan had successfully gotten their setup working, so I examined their circuit to better understand where my setup was going wrong. I compared their wiring and component arrangement with mine and used their working setup as a reference to help troubleshoot and improve my own.

6. Rebuilding the Circuit

During office hours, Dr. Dzula helped me better understand how the stepper motor system worked and guided me through assembling a functioning setup. I began applying what I had learned about breadboard connections, power, and the A4988 driver.

7. Checking the Connections

As we continued rebuilding the circuit, I became more familiar with identifying the different connections and understanding how each component affected the system. This allowed me to troubleshoot the circuit more effectively instead of simply following the tutorial without understanding why each connection was necessary.

8. Testing the Motor and Circuit

After making the necessary changes to the wiring and power connections, I continued testing the system. The stepper motor began responding to the Arduino, showing that the connections and driver were working together correctly.

9. Getting the Motor to Respond

After working through the different wiring and setup issues, I was able to get the stepper motor to respond to the Arduino's commands. This was an important point in the process because I could finally see the system operating as intended.
