Unit 2 - Day 13 - Simple Spinning Scanner

In this project, Ava and I worked with an Arduino Uno, an A4988 stepper motor driver, and a stepper motor to learn how to create a simple spinning scanner. We followed a tutorial, experimented with the hardware and wiring, and worked through several problems before getting the system to function.

Step 1 — Learning the Setup

Ava and I began by following a YouTube tutorial on how to control and operate a stepper motor using an Arduino. We used the tutorial to understand the purpose of the different components and how they were supposed to work together.

Step 2 — Beginning the Build

We began assembling the circuit and following the tutorial's instructions. At this stage, we were focused on understanding the connections between the Arduino, breadboard, driver, and other components needed to control the stepper motor.

Step 3 — Adding a Heat Sink

We attached a heat sink to the driver’s main chip to help dissipate heat and prevent the component from overheating during operation. This helps keep the driver at a safer operating temperature while controlling the stepper motor.

Step 4 — Identifying the Problem

After Ava left, I continued troubleshooting on my own. I eventually disassembled the setup and used ChatGPT to help me understand how the components should be connected and operated. However, my initial attempt still did not work, so I continued investigating the problem.

Step 5 — Using a Working Setup as a Reference

John Deng and Ryan had successfully gotten their setup working, so I examined their circuit to better understand where my setup was going wrong. I compared their wiring and component arrangement with mine and used their working setup as a reference to help troubleshoot and improve my own.

Step 6 — Rebuilding the System

During office hours, Dr. Dzula helped me better understand how the stepper motor system worked and guided me through assembling a functioning setup. Through this process, I learned more about how the connections on a breadboard work, how to properly power the system, and how the A4988 driver communicates between the Arduino and the stepper motor.

Step 7 — Troubleshooting the Connections

As we continued rebuilding the circuit, I became more familiar with identifying the different connections and understanding how each component affected the system. This allowed me to troubleshoot the circuit more effectively instead of simply following the tutorial without understanding why each connection was necessary.

Step 8 — Testing the System

After making the necessary changes to the wiring and power connections, I continued testing the system. The stepper motor was beginning to respond to the Arduino, showing that the connections and driver were working together correctly.

Step 9 — Getting the Motor to Operate

After working through the different wiring and setup issues, I was able to get the stepper motor to respond to the Arduino's commands. This was an important point in the process because I could finally see the system operating as intended.

Step 10 — Working Stepper Motor

The system was now able to successfully power and control the stepper motor. After troubleshooting the circuit and learning how to properly use the A4988 driver, I was able to get the motor to respond to the Arduino's programmed commands. This demonstrated how the Arduino, driver, breadboard, power connections, and stepper motor work together as one system.
